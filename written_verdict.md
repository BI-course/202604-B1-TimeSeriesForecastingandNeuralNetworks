We tested whether our models can predict Microsoft stock better than the naive forecast, which simply assumes tomorrow will be the same as today. We used walk-forward testing, which means the models were always trained on past days and tested on days they had never seen, so time was never mixed up. We repeated this 29 times and scored about 1,781 days. The naive forecast had an average error of 5.79 dollars. Our best model, gradient boosting, had an error of 5.84 dollars, and ridge regression had 5.88 dollars, so both models were slightly worse than the naive forecast. Gradient boosting guessed the direction of the price correctly 52 percent of the time, which is only a tiny step above a coin flip at 50 percent. So our honest answer is no, our models do not beat the naive forecast. This is not a bad result, because stock prices are very hard to predict. Many people are trying to predict them, so easy patterns disappear quickly, and it is better to report this honestly than to pretend the model is great. The client should not trust the 63-day forecast very much. It is almost a flat line at today's price, which only says that no big change is expected, and the range around it gets very wide, which shows how uncertain the future is. For the one-day range, only 76.3 percent of the real results fell inside it, while we aimed for 80 percent, so our model is a bit too confident. We advise the client to use the forecast to understand risk through the width of the range, and not to decide when to buy or sell.
